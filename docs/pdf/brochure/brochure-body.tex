\section{The Problem: Bots Don\u0027t Look Like Bots Anymore}
\index{bots!modern evasion}\index{SSL inspection}\index{compliance}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\newthought{A credential-stuffing campaign looks identical to human login traffic}
at the HTTP layer. The requests are syntactically valid. The user-agent strings are
plausible. Your CDN\u0027s bot-score misses it because the attacker rotates IP addresses
faster than reputation feeds update.

\sidenote{The Verizon DBIR consistently shows that credential abuse accounts for
more than 40\% of breaches. Most credential-stuffing toolkits ship with TLS stacks
that produce distinctive, stable fingerprints --- yet traditional defences never
inspect the handshake.}

Traditional TLS-aware defences --- SSL inspection and decryption --- introduce
significant latency and create a massive compliance exposure: PCI-DSS, HIPAA,
and GDPR all restrict access to decrypted traffic. \ja{} solves this by
enforcing security \emph{before} decryption, operating directly on the
ClientHello handshake.

\section{The JA4 Insight}
\index{JA4!fingerprint}\index{TLS!ClientHello}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\newthought{Before a single byte of encrypted data is exchanged,} every TLS client
announces its capabilities in cleartext. This announcement --- the ClientHello ---
contains the TLS version, cipher suites, and extensions (SNI, ALPN).

Because this information reflects the client\u0027s TLS stack, it is remarkably
stable. Sliver 1.5.x produces the same ClientHello whether it is running from
Frankfurt or Tokyo. Upgrading the \emph{payload} has no effect on the fingerprint.

\sidenote{\textbf{Elite Performance:} The \ja{} Go core processes these
fingerprints in less than \textbf{272 nanoseconds}, enabling theoretical
throughput of \textbf{3.6 million connections per second} per core.}

\section{Architecture: The Ultra-Lite Go Engine}
\index{architecture}\index{Go-native}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\newthought{\ja{} v2.0.0 is a pure Go-native implementation} designed for
extreme resource efficiency and sub-microsecond latency. The system is
distributed as exactly two binaries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\ja{}pd (The Engine)}: A stateless, high-concurrency traffic
        daemon that handles TLS-passthrough at scale.
  \item \textbf{\ja{}p (The Toolset)}: A unified operational CLI for management,
        real-time simulation, and integrated high-speed benchmarking.
\end{itemize}

\sidenote{\ja{}pd has a memory footprint of less than \textbf{12 MB} and
starts in under \textbf{50 ms}, making it ideal for containerised auto-scaling
environments.}
